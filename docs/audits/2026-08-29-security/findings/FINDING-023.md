**Residuals, recorded rather than closed.** (1) The three sibling tests share two blind spots this sprint retired — they match only `routes = [` and enumerate only bare `[env.X]` headers. Their stakes are lower (all three deploy `--env production` only, so a top-level route drift never auto-deploys, and wrangler's `route`/`routes` mutual exclusion turns the singular-assignment drift into a loud config error once `[env.production]` declares `routes`), and reopening them means re-versioning three app releases already changelogged on this branch. **The one worth naming: `apps/oauth/src/__tests__/wrangler-config.test.ts:69`** — oauth's top level *is* production and a bare `wrangler deploy` is the production deploy, so a `[env.production.vars]` sub-table would create a deployable second production-shaped environment the check cannot see: structurally the same hazard as the `[env.preview]` block FINDING-029 deleted in the 2026-08-21 audit. (2) Nothing tests the deploy workflows themselves — deleting `deploy-image-worker.yml`'s Test step, or changing `deploy --env production` to a bare `deploy`, removes the gate or retargets it at the routeless `-dev` worker with no test going red; that is the load-bearing assumption under "test-enforced", and it applies to all nine workers equally. (3) Every test here guards the repo's config, not Cloudflare's stored state: a route or subdomain added through the dashboard is invisible until the next deploy overwrites it.

Two rulings worth carrying: the optional `X-Internal-Caller` shared header was **not** implemented — it would have touched three deploy units, needed a deploy ordering and a provisioned secret, and defends only what the free hostname check already covers, since both callers fetch `https://image-worker/<path>`. And matching text rather than parsing TOML leaves two exotic spellings (a quoted bare key, an inline-table environment) unmatched in every test of this set; recorded in the test's own header rather than chased, because closing it means taking on a TOML parser for a guard whose job is to catch an ordinary edit.
